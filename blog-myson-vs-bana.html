Mỹ Sơn or Bà Nà Hills — Which Day Trip Should You Pick?

One is a thousand years old and nearly silent. The other has a bridge held up by giant stone hands.

These are the two big day trips out of Hội An, and people often try to do both in one trip. If you've only got time for one, they're so different that the choice usually makes itself once you know what you're choosing between.

Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary

About an hour inland, in a valley ringed by mountains, is what's left of the Champa Kingdom — a seafaring civilisation that ruled the central Vietnamese coast for over a thousand years, and whose religion came from Hinduism carried across the sea from India.

The first temple went up here in the 4th century, dedicated to Shiva. It burned down, was rebuilt in brick, and then for the next thousand years successive Champa kings kept adding to it — over 70 structures, each generation building in the style of its own era. Kings came here to perform the rites that confirmed their right to rule.

The thing that stops people is the brickwork. The Cham fired their bricks and stacked them with no mortar in between — no cement, no visible joins — and those walls are still standing sixteen centuries later. Nobody has fully worked out how they did it. Modern restorations using cement have aged worse than the originals.

UNESCO listed it in 1999. It's quiet, a bit melancholy, and there are sections still marked from bomb damage in the war. Go early: the site is hot by 10AM and the tour buses arrive mid-morning.

Pick Mỹ Sơn if

  • You like history and ruins, and you're happy to walk around a site reading about it.
  • You want something quiet.
  • You've got half a day rather than a full one.
  • You'd rather see something most travellers to Vietnam skip.

Bà Nà Hills

Completely the opposite, and that's the appeal. Bà Nà sits at 1,487 metres in the forest about 45 minutes from Đà Nẵng. The French built a hill station up here to escape the coastal heat, and named it after the banana trees they found growing.

You get there by cable car — the network holds four world records, and the ride up takes around 20 minutes through forest and past a waterfall. For a lot of people the cable car is the best part.

The Golden Bridge is the famous one: 150 metres of walkway held up by two enormous stone hands, sitting above the clouds on a clear day. But the site is much bigger than the bridge — a full French Village with a cathedral and cobbled squares, a Gothic castle, flower gardens, a funicular railway, an old French wine cellar cut into the mountain, and an indoor amusement park.

It's a built attraction rather than a historic site, and it doesn't pretend otherwise. The mountain air is genuinely cool, the views over Đà Nẵng are huge, and it's a strange, enjoyable day out.

Pick Bà Nà if

  • You're travelling with kids or teenagers.
  • You want photos — this place is built for them.
  • You want to escape the heat for a day.
  • You'd rather be entertained than educated.

Practical differences

  • Time: Mỹ Sơn is a half day, usually early morning. Bà Nà is a full day.
  • Weather: Bà Nà is often in cloud — a clear day is luck, and fog means no view from the bridge. Mỹ Sơn works in most weather.
  • Walking: Both involve a fair bit. Bà Nà is more, and there are stairs.
  • Best time of day: Both are much better early. Mỹ Sơn for the heat, Bà Nà for the queues.

If you can only do one

If you're the kind of traveller who reads plaques and likes knowing why something was built, go to Mỹ Sơn. If you're travelling with family, or you've had enough temples for one trip, go to Bà Nà. Neither is the wrong answer — they're just not competing for the same day.

Both trips run from Hội An.
