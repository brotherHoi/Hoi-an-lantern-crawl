Option One

Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary

About an hour inland from Hội An, hidden in a valley ringed by mountains, sits what's left of a civilization most travellers have never heard of. Mỹ Sơn was the religious and political heart of the Champa Kingdom — a seafaring people who ruled central Vietnam's coast for over a thousand years.

The first temple went up in the 4th century, dedicated to Shiva. For the next thousand years Champa kings kept adding to the site — over 70 structures, each generation building in the style of its own era, coming here to perform the rites that confirmed their right to rule.

What makes people stop and stare is the brickwork. The Cham stacked fired bricks with no mortar between them — no cement, no visible joins — and the walls are still standing sixteen centuries later. Nobody has fully worked out how. UNESCO listed it in 1999, calling the monuments unique and without equal in Southeast Asia.

Option Two

Bà Nà Hills

Bà Nà Hills is the complete opposite of Mỹ Sơn, and that's the point. Where one is ancient and quiet, this is theatrical and built for wonder. It sits at 1,487 metres in the forest about 45 minutes from Đà Nẵng, named by the French after the banana trees they found when they came up looking for cool mountain air.

You get there by cable car, and the ride alone is worth it — the network holds four world records, and takes around 20 minutes climbing through forest and past a waterfall on the way up.

The Golden Bridge is what most people come for: 150 metres of walkway held up by two enormous stone hands, floating above the clouds on a clear day. But the park is much bigger than one bridge — a full French Village with a cathedral and cobbled squares, a Gothic castle, flower gardens, a funicular railway, an old French wine cellar carved into the mountain, and a fantasy amusement park. The mountain air is cool and the views over Đà Nẵng are enormous.
